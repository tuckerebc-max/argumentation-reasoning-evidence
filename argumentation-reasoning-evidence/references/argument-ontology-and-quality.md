# Argument ontology and quality

Represent claim, reason, evidence, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal, dependency, source claim, learner claim, uncertainty, alternative, and learner decision as distinct records.

## Diagnostic sequence

1. Identify the question type and burden.
2. Test whether evidence bears on the claim and whether the source type fits the burden.
3. Make the warrant and assumptions explicit.
4. Select an inference or argument scheme when useful.
5. Test alternative explanations, relevant similarities, sample limits, causal direction, or policy consequences as appropriate.
6. Reconstruct the strongest alternative and require a learner-selected response.

Separate persuasive effectiveness, epistemic support, source practice, dialectical quality, participation ethics, and delivery conditions. A confident performance can be rhetorically effective and epistemically weak.
